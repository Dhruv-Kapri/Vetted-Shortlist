# Listings vs the DLD register

Generated by `uv run python -m scripts.dld_check > /tmp/out.md`, then pasted between this header and the reading (header and reading written by hand).

**Method.** Two DLD open-data pulls. Rents: 2026-10-04 to 2026-11-04, 66 rent rows for Princess Tower (64 Flats,
2 Shops dropped) and 27 for Binghatti Heights. Sales: 2026-01-01 to 2026-11-05, all Dubai Marina sales (1,814 rows;
39 for Princess Tower). The Binghatti Heights (JVC) sales export is not in the repo yet, so that building shows
"not exported". Listings are the live portal rentals and sales and the registered deals shown on listing pages.
A listing matches a DLD row when its size, converted from sqft to sqm (1 sqm = 10.7639 sqft), is within +-5% of
the DLD ACTUAL_AREA. Rooms are not used for rents because 96% of DLD rent rows have none; sales do carry rooms
(`1 B/R` etc.) and are grouped by them. Sales are cleaned to ready flats, deduped on TRANSACTION_NUMBER, and
partial-share or odd transfers are dropped (PROCEDURE_AREA differs from ACTUAL_AREA by more than 1%, or value under
AED 300,000); the Q3 note lists the counts.

**Limits.**
- Rents cover one month only. Medians with n under about 10 are fragile.
- DLD has no unit number, so every match is size-level, not unit-level.
- ANNUAL_AMOUNT for a few rows differs from CONTRACT_AMOUNT (multi-cheque or part-period quirks); we use ANNUAL_AMOUNT as given.
- The listing-page deals column uses the 6-month window of `building_check.py`; DLD rents are 1 month, so they are not the same population.
- 3-bed Binghatti Heights flats (about 125 sqm) have no size-matching DLD rent in the window, so nothing can be judged there.
- Sales: a DLD median per bedroom count mixes sizes (Princess Tower 3-beds run 186 to 223 sqm), so the "% of DLD median" is
  coarse; size-matched medians and per-sqm medians sit next to it. No unit, floor or view is known on either side.

## Reading

**Q1.** Both claims survive, with caveats.
- "Cheapest of the 55 audited listings is 7% under the listing-page median": reproduced exactly. That listing
  (169274661, 1,184 sqft, AED 110,000) has 24 listing-page deals within +-10 sqft over 183 days, median 118,500,
  so the ask is -7%. (An earlier version of this file tested a different listing, the cheapest of all 217,
  159662647 at AED 105,000 and 1,298 sqft, and wrongly said the n=24 / 118,500 figures could not be reproduced.
  That one has n=10, median 113,500, also -7%. Different listing, different sample, same conclusion.)
- DLD's own contracts for the audited listing's size (+-5% of 110 sqm) are few: New n=2 (AED 115,000 and 155,000),
  Renewed n=5 (median 126,000, range 115,000 to 150,000). Both sit above the 110,000 ask, but n=2 and n=5 cannot
  confirm a median; read them as "not contradicting", not as a second proof. For the cheapest 2-bed overall
  the DLD New median is 117,500 (n=8), so the ask is -11%. Neither case is bait-level.
- "No listing under 75% of registered deals": holds against DLD. Among the 88 of 137 Princess Tower rentals and
  95 of 263 Binghatti Heights rentals with at least 3 size-matched New contracts, the lowest ask is 89% and 91%
  of the DLD median. The other listings could not be judged; Binghatti Heights coverage is thin (27 DLD rows).
- Asking medians sit close to or above DLD New medians for common sizes (Princess Tower 1-bed 85,000 vs 77,500;
  2-bed 120,000 vs 117,500; 3-bed asks run well above the DLD median). Renewed rents are not consistently cheaper here.

**Q3. Sales (Princess Tower only; Binghatti Heights not exported).** No bait, and asks lean high.
- Cleaning: 39 rows, 33 kept; 2 partial-share rows and 4 rows under AED 300,000 (163, 40,505, 54,581, 131,418) dropped.
- No live sale ask is under 75% of the DLD median for its bedroom count. The lowest asks are 92% (1-bed), 87% (2-bed)
  and 95% (3-bed) of the median. Asks over 125% of the median are common: 4 of 26 one-beds, 8 of 56 two-beds, 7 of 21
  three-beds. Ask medians are 3% (1-bed), 5% (2-bed) and 11% (3-bed) above DLD medians, so the market is asking more
  than it has been closing, as is usual, not less.
- Listing-page sample vs DLD sample: the expected headline (listing pages show only about 4 two-bed sales vs
  DLD's 8) does not hold as stated. Listing pages show 9 two-bed-sized sales in 12 months (about 4 of them are the
  ~1,180 sqft type, the rest ~1,300 and ~1,730 sqft) against 7 DLD two-bed sales in 10 months after cleaning
  (11 raw rows; the other 4 are the half-share and sub-AED 300,000 transfers). Even per 10 months the counts are similar.
  Matching on date and amount, 5 of the 7 DLD two-bed sales (12 of 14 one-beds, 9 of 10 three-beds) already appear
  on listing pages. So the listing-page deals are a near-complete view of registered sales, and
  `building_check.py`'s sale table (judged on 3+ page deals per size) is not undersampled relative to DLD in any way that
  changes a claim. The sample is small in both sources: a handful of two-bed sales per year.
- Caveat: the DLD rows carry no unit, so a cheap DLD sale could be a lower floor or an unrenovated unit that no live ad matches.

**Q2.** The stale-ad belief is not supported, and the proxy cannot test it well.
- Loose match (any size-matched New contract): Princess Tower stale 95% vs fresh 100%; Binghatti Heights stale 86%
  vs fresh 77%. Within bedroom counts the rates are the same or fresh is higher (Princess Tower 100% in every group
  with matches; Binghatti Heights 1-bed 98% vs 98%, 2-bed 87% vs 95%). The Binghatti Heights gap is a mix effect: more
  of its fresh ads are 3-beds, which have no DLD match at all. So the loose proxy does not discriminate; the base rate is
  near 100% for common sizes.
- Strict match (also rent within +-10% of asking): Princess Tower stale 49% (27 of 55) vs fresh 77% (63 of 82), and
  the gap holds in every bedroom group. Binghatti Heights stale 70% (67 of 96) vs fresh 60% (101 of 167), but
  within bedroom counts it is 89% vs 87% and 52% vs 47%, which is noise-sized. If stale ads meant "already let", the
  strict rate should be higher for stale; at Princess Tower it is lower. A more natural reading is that stale
  ads are priced above what is currently being signed.
- Not shown: that stale ads are still available. Not shown either: that they have gone. A contract in the same
  size band can be any other unit, a one-month window cannot say when the contract was for this unit, and stale ads
  cluster in particular sizes. Treat the belief as untested rather than refuted; a unit-level source (listing
  removal dates over time) would be needed.
